### Licensing notes for future deck work
- Share-alike files here (mw_thin_thick_disk_diagram, gaia_allsky, origin_of_elements) are fine to
  display, but any derivative figure built from them inherits BY-SA. The SDSS files are plain CC BY,
  so prefer those when you intend to annotate.
- **Not found, and why:** no redistributable SEGUE sky-footprint figure exists on current SDSS sites
  (the canonical one is Yanny et al. 2009, AJ, not open-licensed); no redistributable Type Ia vs
  core-collapse *timescale* diagram (the good ones are in ARA&A / MNRAS reviews). A hand-drawn
  timescale cartoon is the honest answer for that slide.
- **Licence vs fair use — do not conflate them** (GN 2026-09-02). "No licence permits redistribution"
  is not "we may not use it". A licence grants rights you would otherwise lack; fair use is a right
  you already have. A single cited figure from a scientific paper, in a non-commercial course, is
  the textbook fair-use case and is what citation exists for. Prefer an explicit open licence when
  one exists, because it also covers people who fork this repo — but absence of one is not a veto.
- **Explicitly open here:** ATLAS (CC BY 4.0, granted verbatim in the page footer), SDSS (Image Use
  Policy), the two CEERS papers (CC BY 4.0), the Wikimedia and ESA files (CC BY-SA variants).
  Used under fair use with citation: the Bovy, Rix & Hogg 2012 figures. Also available on the same
  basis if wanted later: CMS diphoton figures, the Bennett et al. dead-salmon fMRI figure, and the
  BICEP2/Planck figures — all previously and wrongly ruled out here on licence grounds alone.

## strateva2003_fig3ab_halpha_gaussian_fits_crop.png
- **Title:** Figure 3, panels a and b, "Example Gaussian fits. The original spectrum is binned to ~210 km/s pixels with the sum of Gaussians fit overlaid (thick line)... The sum of broad components attributed to disk emission are shown above with a solid line, while all broad components are given separately with dashed lines... a) Three broad-component fit. b) Three broad-Gaussian fit, with a central broad Hα component." Top row cropped from the 2×2 figure (1167×572 PNG). Added 2026-09-17.
- **Source page:** https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0307357
- **File downloaded:** https://arxiv.org/e-print/astro-ph/0307357 (author's LaTeX source tarball; `Strateva.fig3.ps` rendered with `gs -sDEVICE=png16m -r150 -dEPSCrop`, top row cropped with PIL)
- **Credit:** Strateva, I. V., et al. (2003), "Double-peaked Low-Ionization Emission Lines in Active Galactic Nuclei," AJ 126, 1720 (arXiv:astro-ph/0307357), Fig. 3
- **License:** arXiv non-exclusive distribution license; underlying journal copyright is AJ/AAS. Single figure from a cited paper in a non-commercial university lecture: fair use, attributed.
- **Shows:** what the paper's fit actually looks like: narrow lines ([O I] 6300/6364, [N II], [S II], narrow Hα) fit and subtracted, then two to four broad Gaussians for the disk line; panel b's blue-edge bump near −12,000 km/s is the [O I] feature we left in the continuum window.
